The Qur’an made for the library of Sultan Ahmad al-Mansur

Le Coran de la bibliothèque du sultan Ahmad al-Mansur (Escurial) · مصحف خزانة السلطان أحمد المنصور

Wednesday 13 Rabiʿ II 1008 AH · c. 2 November 1599 CE

In the reign of Ahmad al-Mansur

Qalun from Nafiʿ

It is known as the Qur’an of Mawlay Zaydan, because it was in the library of al-Mansur’s son, Sultan Zaydan, when the Spanish seized it in 1612 CE, and entered the Escorial with the rest of his books.

Its colophon is a full page in gold on a lapis blue ground, and describes the ink: «بالمداد المقام من فائق العنبر المتعاهد السقيا بالعبير...», “with ink made of the finest ambergris, steeped again and again in perfume...”. François Déroche and Nuria Martínez de Castilla see a clear Ottoman influence in its binding and decoration. Between its lines, small gold ṣāds mark the places of pause set out by Muhammad al-Habti (d. 930 AH).

From the colophon in gold on lapis blue (fol. 264r)

...برسم الخزانة العلية الكريمة النبوية الحسنية الإمامية الأحمدية المنصورية... ووافق تمامه يوم الأربعاء الثالث عشر من ربيع الثاني عام ثمانية وألف بجامع الديوان الكريم من قصور الإمامة العلية...

...for the exalted, noble, prophetic, Hasanid, imamic, Ahmadi, Mansuri library... Its completion fell on Wednesday the thirteenth of Rabiʿ II of the year one thousand and eight, in the mosque of the noble Diwan in the palaces of the exalted imamate...

Our reading from the image of the folio. The text is long, so we have kept only the passages naming the library and the date. 13 Rabiʿ II 1008 AH corresponds to about 2 November 1599 CE.
