Idrisid Qur’ans

Dated Qur’ans with a known copyist or patron from the Idrisid period: the ones that have come down to us, and the ones that can be browsed online today.

No dated Qur’an from the Idrisid period is known today, nor one that names its copyist or owner. In these centuries Qur’ans were written in the early Abbasid scripts on horizontal parchment, and their colophons rarely gave a date or a name. When Idris II founded Fez he prayed: “I only wished in building it that You be worshipped in it and Your Book be recited in it.” This page presents what may go back to this period, and the limits of that attribution.

Criteria

A Qur’an enters the first list when three things hold:

  • The date of copying is stated explicitly in its colophon or endowment deed, not inferred from its style.
  • Its owner is known: the copyist, if named, or the person for whom or for whose library it was made.
  • It was demonstrably copied in the Idrisid lands of the far Maghrib.

The Idrisids ruled northern Morocco from 172 AH / 788 CE, and Idris II founded Fez. In the fourth century AH their realm shrank between the Fatimids and the Umayyads of al-Andalus, until it ended in 375 AH / 985 CE.

Limits of this guide

We found no dated Qur’an, or Qur’an with a known copyist, from the Idrisid period in the published studies. The oldest known dated Maghribi Qur’ans are two leaves in the Museum of Turkish and Islamic Arts in Istanbul, dated Rajab 398 AH and Safar 432 AH; both are later than the fall of the Idrisids and their place of copying is unknown. The “Qur’an of ʿUqba b. Nafiʿ” mentioned in Moroccan sources is attributed to the time of the conquest, not to the Idrisids; Mawlay ʿAbd Allah the Alaouite sent it as a gift to Medina in 1155 AH. The Qarawiyyin Library holds fragments and near-complete Qur’ans in early Abbasid scripts that have not yet been studied, and some of them may go back to this period.
