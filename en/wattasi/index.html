Wattasid Qur’ans

Dated Qur’ans with a known copyist or patron from the Wattasid period in Fez: the ones that have come down to us, and the ones that can be browsed online today.

The Wattasid period is one of the poorest in studied Qur’ans: in the published studies available to us we found no Qur’an of this period that names its copyist, its date and its place together. Yet it was in this period that Muhammad b. Abi Jumʿa al-Habti (d. 930 AH in Fez) set out the pausal marks that Maghribi Qur’ans have followed to this day. The most notable survival is the Green Qur’an in the National Library in Rabat, which is undated. This page presents what we found and shows where others may be hoped for.

Criteria

A Qur’an enters the first list when three things hold:

  • The date of copying is stated explicitly in its colophon or endowment deed, not inferred from its style.
  • Its owner is known: the copyist, if named, or the person for whom or for whose library it was made.
  • It was demonstrably copied in the Wattasid lands, that is, Fez and its region.

The Wattasids ruled Fez as sultans from 876 AH / 1472 CE to 961 AH / 1554 CE; for about half a century before that they had been viziers and regents of the Marinids, and the Qur’ans of those years are on the Marinid page. The Saadians rose in the Sous in 916 AH / 1510 CE and entered Marrakesh in 931 AH / 1525 CE, so the south became theirs, and their Qur’ans are on the Saadian page.

Paper dyed entirely green, so that none of the natural colour of the paper shows; writing in silver; modern binding
Attribution
To the Wattasid period, in the “Discover Islamic Art” database of Museum With No Frontiers (MWNF) and by Khabta

One of the few Maghribi Qur’ans written on paper coloured throughout. Mohamed Abdelhafid Khabta sees it as a witness to the level reached in this period in making and dyeing paper and preparing inks, and pairs it with two other Qur’ans on coloured paper: Qarawiyyin 5 and Arabe 389 in Paris.

Undated, and neither its copyist nor its owner is known. Its attribution to the Wattasid period rests on its style.

Limits of this guide

In the published studies available to us we found no Wattasid Qur’an that names its copyist, its date and its place together. The Qarawiyyin Library, the National Library and the Royal Library most likely hold Qur’ans of this period that have been neither studied nor digitised; the catalogue of the Qarawiyyin Library by Mohammed al-ʿAbid al-Fasi (1979–1989) is the first place to look. We welcome any pointer to a published dated Wattasid Qur’an.
